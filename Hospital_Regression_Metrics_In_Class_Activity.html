Every prediction is 10 patients away from the actual value, even though some errors are positive and some are negative.

MAE Solution

MAE = (10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10) ÷ 5 = 10 patients

Interpretation: The model's predictions are off by 10 patients on average.

MSE Solution

MSE = (100 + 100 + 100 + 100 + 100) ÷ 5 = 100

Interpretation: The average squared error is 100. MSE is in squared units and gives larger errors more influence.

RMSE Solution

RMSE = √100 = 10 patients

Interpretation: RMSE returns the result to the original unit—patients—while retaining the squared-error approach.

MAE and RMSE are both 10 here because every error has exactly the same size. If one error were much larger, RMSE would react more strongly.

MAPE Solution

MAPE = (10.00 + 8.33 + 7.14 + 6.25 + 5.56) ÷ 5

MAPE = 37.28 ÷ 5 ≈ 7.46%

Interpretation: The model's predictions are off by approximately 7.46% on average.

The error is always 10 patients, but the percentage differs because 10 patients is a larger percentage of 100 than of 180.

R² Solution

1. Mean Actual Patient Count

Mean = (100 + 120 + 140 + 160 + 180) ÷ 5 = 140 patients

A simple baseline would therefore predict 140 patients every day.

3. Residual Squared Error

100 + 100 + 100 + 100 + 100 = 500

4. R² Calculation

R² = 1 − (500 ÷ 4,000)

R² = 1 − 0.125 = 0.875 = 87.5%

Interpretation: The regression model explains about 87.5% of the observed variation in daily ER patient counts compared with simply predicting the average of 140 patients every day.
Important: R² = 87.5% does not mean the model is “87.5% accurate.” R² describes explained variation.

Key Takeaway

We used the same actual values and the same predictions for every metric, but each metric gave us different information:

  • MAE: How far off are we on average?
  • MSE: What is the average squared error?
  • RMSE: What is the error in the original unit while giving larger errors extra influence?
  • MAPE: What percentage off are we on average?
  • R²: How much observed variation does the model explain compared with predicting the mean?

The metrics are not five different ways of saying the same thing. They answer different questions about the same model.
