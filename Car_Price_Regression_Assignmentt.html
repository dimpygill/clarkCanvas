Purpose: What Is Regression?

Regression is a type of supervised machine learning used to predict a numerical value from input information (features). For example, a car’s age and mileage can help a model estimate its selling price in dollars. Regression learns patterns from examples where the actual price is known, then uses those patterns to estimate prices for cars it has not seen before. The prediction is an estimate, not a guaranteed selling price.

5. Evaluation metrics: what, how, and why?

MetricHow it is calculatedWhy it matters
MAE (Mean Absolute Error)Take the absolute value of each actual-minus-predicted error, add them, divide by number of cars.Shows typical prediction error in dollars. Lower is better.
RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)Square each error, average the squares, then take the square root.Penalizes large pricing mistakes more strongly. Lower is better.
R² (R-squared)Compare the model's total squared prediction error with the total squared deviations from the test-set mean price.Shows how much variation is explained relative to a mean-price benchmark. Higher is better; values can be negative.

Worked example: Actual prices = $10,000, $15,000, $20,000; predicted prices = $11,000, $14,000, $18,000. Absolute errors = $1,000, $1,000, $2,000. MAE = (1000+1000+2000)/3 = $1,333.33. RMSE = √[(1,000,000+1,000,000+4,000,000)/3] = $1,414.21. The actual mean is $15,000, so R² = 1 − 6,000,000/50,000,000 = 0.88.

Manual calculation requirement: Select at least three of your own test records and demonstrate the steps for calculating their MAE and RMSE. Explain why a calculation on three selected rows may differ from the metrics calculated on the entire test set. Show how R² is computed or interpreted using your full test-set results.

Why calculate metrics yourself? A prediction alone cannot tell you whether a model is reliable. Metrics quantify error, reveal costly large mistakes, and allow evidence-based comparison. A model with a lower MAE is preferable when typical dollar error matters most; a model with a lower RMSE may be preferable when unusually large mistakes are especially costly. R² helps assess improvement over predicting a constant average, but does not tell you the dollar error.
